Unit 1: Numbers and their operations

Lesson One: Real Numbers

From Rational ($\mathbb{Q}$) to Real ($\mathbb{R}$)

Part 1

Review: Sets of Numbers

Natural Numbers $\mathbb{N}$

$\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,3,4,...\}$

Integers $\mathbb{Z}$

$\mathbb{Z}=\{...,-2,-1,0,1,2,...\}$

Rational Numbers $\mathbb{Q}$

$\mathbb{Q}=\{\frac{a}{b}, a \in \mathbb{Z}, b \in \mathbb{Z}, b \ne 0\}$

Examples: $\frac{3}{4}, -0.2, 13\%, \sqrt{36}, 0.\overline{7}$

Part 2

The Set of Irrational Numbers ($\mathbb{Q}'$)

Definition

An irrational number is a number that cannot be written in the form $\frac{a}{b}$ (where a, b are integers and $b \neq 0$).

Note: $\mathbb{Q} \cap \mathbb{Q}' = \emptyset$ (They are disjoint sets).

1. Non-Perfect Squares

Square roots of numbers that are not perfect squares.

  • $\sqrt{2}$ (No rational number squared = 2)
  • $\sqrt{5}, \sqrt{18}, \sqrt{25}+\sqrt{7}$

2. Non-Perfect Cubes

Cube roots of numbers that are not perfect cubes.

  • $\sqrt[3]{4}$ (No rational number cubed = 4)
  • $\sqrt[3]{-9}, \sqrt[3]{7}, \sqrt[3]{25}$

3. The Ratio $\pi$

Pi ($\pi$) is the ratio of circle circumference to diameter.

$\pi \approx 3.14159...$

Note: $\frac{22}{7}$ and $3.14$ are just approximations, not exact values.
Example 1: Which of the following are Rational ($\mathbb{Q}$) vs Irrational ($\mathbb{Q}'$)?

1) $0.73$    2) $\sqrt{8}$    3) $\pi+2$    4) $\sqrt[3]{-8}$    5) $\sqrt{25}+\sqrt{7}$
  1. $0.73$: Rational ($\frac{73}{100}$).
  2. $\sqrt{8}$: Irrational (8 is not a perfect square).
  3. $\pi+2$: Irrational (Contains $\pi$).
  4. $\sqrt[3]{-8}$: Rational (Equals $-2$).
  5. $\sqrt{25}+\sqrt{7}$: Irrational (Equals $5 + \sqrt{7}$, and 7 is not a perfect square).
Part 3

Approximating Irrational Numbers

Any irrational number lies between two rational numbers (often two consecutive integers).

Example: Estimate $\sqrt{18}$

Find two consecutive integers between which $\sqrt{18}$ lies.

1. Find perfect squares surrounding 18:

$$ 16 < 18 < 25 $$

2. Take the square root:

$$ \sqrt{16} < \sqrt{18} < \sqrt{25} $$

$$ 4 < \sqrt{18} < 5 $$

Since 18 is closer to 16 than 25, $\sqrt{18}$ is closer to 4 ($\approx 4.24$).

Part 4

The Set of Real Numbers ($\mathbb{R}$)

$\mathbb{R} = \mathbb{Q} \cup \mathbb{Q}'$

Real Numbers ($\mathbb{R}$)
Irrational ($\mathbb{Q}'$)

$\sqrt{2}, \pi, \sqrt[3]{5}$

Rational ($\mathbb{Q}$)
Integers ($\mathbb{Z}$)
Natural ($\mathbb{N}$) 0, 1, 2...
Practice: Put the appropriate symbol ($\in, \notin, \subset, \not\subset$):
1) $\sqrt{2} \dots \mathbb{Q}$ 2) $1.5 \dots \mathbb{R}$ 3) $\{3, \sqrt[3]{4}\} \dots \mathbb{Q}$ 4) $\sqrt{-4} \dots \mathbb{R}$
  1. $\notin$ (Irrational)
  2. $\in$ (Real)
  3. $\not\subset$ (Because $\sqrt[3]{4}$ is not rational)
  4. $\notin$ (Square root of negative number is undefined in $\mathbb{R}$)
Part 5

Solving Equations

Example A: Solution in $\mathbb{Q}$

Solve: $5x^2 - 3 = 7$

$5x^2 = 7 + 3$

$5x^2 = 10$

$x^2 = 2$

$x = \pm\sqrt{2}$

Condition: If $x \in \mathbb{Q}$, S.S = $\emptyset$ (because $\sqrt{2} \notin \mathbb{Q}$)

Condition: If $x \in \mathbb{R}$, S.S = $\{-\sqrt{2}, \sqrt{2}\}$

Example B: No Solution

Solve: $3x^2 + 5 = -4$

$3x^2 = -4 - 5$

$3x^2 = -9$

$x^2 = -3$

$x = \pm\sqrt{-3}$

Result: $\sqrt{-3} \notin \mathbb{R}$

S.S = $\emptyset$

Example C: Cubic Equation

Solve: $\frac{1}{8}x^3 = 1$

$x^3 = 1 \div \frac{1}{8}$

$x^3 = 8$

$x = \sqrt[3]{8}$

$x = 2$

S.S = $\{2\}$

Part 6

Applications & Geometry

📐 Geometry

Square Area: If Area = $29 cm^2$.

Side Length $L = \sqrt{29}$ cm.

$\sqrt{29}$ is Irrational ($\mathbb{Q}'$).


Diagonal: If side = 6cm.

Diagonal = $\sqrt{6^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{72}$ cm.

🐚 Nature (Golden Ratio)

The Golden Ratio appears in nature (e.g., shells).

Value $\approx 1.618...$

Exact form: $\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$

This is an Irrational number.

Creative Thinking: Find an irrational number between $0.\overline{3}$ and $0.\overline{6}$.

$0.\overline{3} \approx 0.333...$

$0.\overline{6} \approx 0.666...$

We need a number that does not repeat and does not end between 0.33 and 0.66.

Possible Answer: $0.414213...$ (which is $\sqrt{2}-1$) or simply $\sqrt{0.2}$ etc. Or explicitly construct one: $0.404004000...$
